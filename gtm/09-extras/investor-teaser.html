Rep Today

A micro-workout app for iOS. It opens to a ready bodyweight session.

Open the app. The workout is already there.

The product in three sentences.

For busy, desk-bound adults who keep skipping movement because starting a workout takes too many steps, Rep Today is the iOS app that opens to a complete bodyweight session with one dominant Start button.

Unlike catalog apps that make you browse and configure-first generators that interview you before you can move, Rep Today never asks a single question between opening the app and starting.

It can make that promise because a deterministic engine is designed to build every session on your phone, offline, in under 100 milliseconds (an engine spec from the PRD; a real-device benchmark is a pre-publication gate and has not yet been run), so there is no server, no login, no quiz, and no paywall standing between you and your first rep.

The wedge: an empty quadrant.

Four attributes, held at once. Instant: the session is on screen at open, generated in under 100 milliseconds per the same PRD spec (device benchmark pending). Offline: built on the phone, no server. Question-free: nothing to answer before Start. Never paywalled: free means every workout, forever.

A competitive teardown dated 2026-07-15 found that no researched competitor combines this stack (competitors-additional.md, covering Seven, Bend, Wakeout, and pliability, on top of earlier research into Nike Training Club, Down Dog, Freeletics, Peloton, Apple Fitness+, and others). The lowest-friction product found anywhere in the research, Wakeout, still advertises "Four taps. Under 30 seconds" and asks outcome, location, and position before starting.

This is a researched observation as of that date, not a moat. Parts of the stack can be copied; the claim is only that the quadrant is empty today.

Sources: internal teardown competitors-additional.md (fetches 2026-07-15); wakeout.app; seven.app; downdogapp.com/faq.

Traction, stated plainly.

Zero users. Zero downloads. Zero revenue. Zero testimonials. The app has not been submitted to the App Store. That is the whole traction story, and this document does not dress it up.

What exists is the build. 667 of 667 tests pass. The MVP core loop is implemented end to end: the deterministic session engine, the AI policy Programmer (on-device heuristics that tune a per-user session policy; the AI never generates a workout and is never on the start path), StoreKit 2 subscriptions, CloudKit private sync, and HealthKit writes. The rebrand from FitSnack to Rep Today is complete and the identifiers are locked, with bundle root com.reptoday.app.

The market, without the fantasy.

Per install, health and fitness monetizes better than any other app category: median revenue per install of $0.48 at day 14, and median year-1 realized value of $35.64 per payer (revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps). The same market is brutally concentrated: the top 10% of apps capture 92.6% of category revenue, and median revenue per newly launched subscription app fell 22% year over year (adapty.io/blog/health-fitness-app-subscription-benchmarks). The ungated free tier is a deliberate bet against the better-monetizing model: freemium apps convert a median 2.1% at day 35, against 10.7% for hard paywalls (revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026).

The plan spends nothing on acquisition, by verdict rather than by default: paid install ads are structurally unavailable pre-launch and tiny iOS budgets buy aggregated noise, so first signal comes from organic short-form video running a pre-registered message matrix, plus a first-party waitlist, with launch posts and App Store search on top. The "micro workout" whitespace ("micro workout" has only two apps ranking with the phrase in their titles, both with too few ratings to display a score (itunes.apple.com/search?term=micro+workout)) is a costless option on top of that, not a reach mechanism: its search volume is unmeasured and may be near zero.

An honest model on these inputs tops out under $30,000 gross in the optimistic year-1 case; the base case is roughly $3,700. This is not a year-1 income story. The first 1,000 downloads are a retention experiment, and the whole bet is falsifiable on the schedule below.

When to walk away.

The full thesis carries nine kill criteria: K0, a marketing-side signal that fires pre-launch if a pre-registered 14-day organic message test produces no signal, plus K1-K8, instrumented before launch and checked on pooled cohorts with minimum sample sizes, at fixed week-8 and week-12 review dates, after at least one fix iteration. The four sharpest post-launch:

Signal Expected band, first 90 days Walk away
K1 · First session 45-60% of onboarding starts complete one session (canonical denominator: installs with onboarding_started, per the thesis) Below 35% after two fix iterations
K2 · D7 return 12-20% (category median 8.5-10%) At or below 10%, the top of the median band, on pooled cohorts at both the week-8 and week-12 reviews
K7 · Free to paid 1.5-3% early, building toward 4% (freemium median 2.1% at day 35) Below 1% at month 3
K8 · Wedge comprehension Observed first runs: users see the ready session and press Start; unprompted descriptions reach for "it's already there when you open it" Over 50% of rubric-coded first runs (25+ observed, coded by a non-founder against a pre-registered rubric) hunt for a workout list, or under 30% of unprompted descriptions mention the ready-on-open behavior

Any one criterion firing is a stop-and-diagnose event. K1 or K8 firing at all is the walk-away signal: it means the premise failed, not the execution.

Category benchmarks: sendbird.com/blog/app-retention-benchmarks-broken-down-by-industry; uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks; revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026.

The ask.

No round is being raised in this document, and no check is being solicited. This is a watch-this-space teaser: the product is built, the experiment is cheap, and the falsification schedule is fixed.

The raise trigger, stated: a round opens only if the 90-day review clears with retention above the category medians and no kill criterion fired, and it would fund what founder time cannot - an Android build for the majority of the target audience that iOS-only concedes, and full-time focus. It would not fund paid acquisition, which the plan's own arithmetic shows is underwater at these unit economics ($50-$190 per payer against $35.64 year-1 value).

  • Before any public launch: a moderated TestFlight cohort of 20 to 50 testers measures K1 and K8 directly, against a pre-registered rubric, and the message matrix runs its pre-registered 14-day organic cadence (K0).
  • First 90 days after launch: every kill criterion is measured from the first cohort onward, and the 90-day review is published publicly within two weeks of the day-90 mark.
  • Cash required: approximately zero beyond the $99 per year Apple developer account.

If the wedge survives its own kill criteria, the next document will contain data instead of claims. If it does not, there will not be a next document.
