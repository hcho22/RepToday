# Judge Scorecard: Differentiation

Judge theme: differentiation.
I score only on how sharply each pitch separates Rep Today from the researched competitor field, claims unclaimed ground, resists copy-paste imitation, and builds a memorable name-plus-hero brand.
Fact-accuracy penalties apply on top where earned.

## Rubric (weights sum to 100)

| Criterion | Weight | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Field distinctiveness | 30 | How clearly the positioning stands apart from the researched competitors (Peloton, NTC, Apple Fitness+, Freeletics, Down Dog, Seven, Centr, 30 Day Fitness, FITsnack). |
| Unowned quadrant | 25 | Whether the pitch claims territory the research shows nobody currently owns, versus ground already contested. |
| Wedge sharpness | 25 | Copy-resistance: could a competitor paste this claim into their own copy without lying or rebuilding their product? |
| Name+hero memorability | 20 | Whether the name, listing name, and hero line combine into one brand a tired person can recall a week later. |

## Per-pitch notes

### Pitch 1: Friction Is the Enemy - 7.55

This is the only pitch whose claimed quadrant is verifiably empty in the research: no researched competitor combines instant-open, offline, question-free, and never-paywalled, and even Wakeout (the closest) still asks three questions before starting.
The wedge is architecture-backed (on-device deterministic generation in under 100ms), so a competitor pasting "opens to a ready workout" into their copy would be lying until they rebuild their product; the pitch honestly concedes the perceived-speed part is fakeable via caching, which caps sharpness at 7.
Weakness: several load-bearing competitor claims (Wakeout, Down Dog, Freeletics onboarding behavior) cite competitors-additional.md, which is outside the research files I was given, so I could not verify them; and keeping "Rest Tomorrow" leaves a hero that is plain rather than ownable, relying on the rhyme for memorability.

### Pitch 2: Identity and Discipline - 6.45

The forgiveness mechanics (rolling score, celebrated Return, Re-entry Ramp) give it a genuinely sharp wedge that streak-based competitors cannot copy without redesigning, and the name-to-hero coherence (Rep Today -> Move Every Day) is tight.
But "Become someone who moves" is Atomic Habits boilerplate any competitor could paste into an ad tomorrow, and the review-mining file shows indie builders already market "no streak anxiety," so the quadrant is contested on both flanks.
Fact caution: it labels Seven, Peloton, and Freeletics as "streak-and-badge apps that punish a missed day," a characterization not supported anywhere in the provided research (Seven's visible reviews are positive and no Peloton streak complaint was mined); that unsupported competitor claim cost it in field distinctiveness.

### Pitch 3: The Anti-Fitness-App - 6.25

"The fitness app for people who hate fitness apps" is the punchiest hero line in the tournament and the calm-software framing does separate it from every major researched competitor.
But the pitch itself concedes the fatal differentiation flaw: the slogan layer ("no streaks, no quiz, no locked door") is copyable in a weekend, and the HN evidence shows builders already marketing exactly this anti-streak ground, so the wedge is the bluntest of the four.
It also asserts competitor feature sets ("Unlike Seven, Down Dog, Freeletics, or 30 Day Fitness, it has no streaks, no badges...") beyond what the provided research documents, and "the X for people who hate X" is a decades-old trope, so the memorability is borrowed rather than owned.

### Pitch 4: Audience-First - 6.70

"A floor, a wall, and five minutes" is the most ownable single line in the tournament: concrete, product-true, and unlike anything in the researched field, which earns the top memorability score.
But the quadrant it claims (micro home workouts for people with no gear) is demonstrably occupied: the name-collisions file shows the abandoned-name collision app FITsnack already sells "daily micro home workouts," and the proposed listing name "Rep Today: 5-Min Home Workouts" dives into the most generic, crowded search phrase in the category, trading the brand's distinctiveness for SEO.
The bodyweight wedge is also shared with a large no-equipment app genre, so competitors can truthfully copy most of the claim, keeping sharpness at 6.

## Winner and margin

**Winner: Pitch 1 (Friction Is the Enemy), 7.55 weighted.**
Margin: 0.85 points over Pitch 4 (6.70), with Pitch 2 (6.45) and Pitch 3 (6.25) trailing.
Pitch 1 wins because it is the only pitch whose core claim sits in a quadrant the research shows is actually empty and whose truth depends on architecture competitors do not have.
No pitch triggered hard penalties: none makes health claims, none uses streak or badge language positively, and all four correctly flag trademark clearance as unverified; Pitches 2 and 3 took soft deductions for unsupported competitor characterizations.
