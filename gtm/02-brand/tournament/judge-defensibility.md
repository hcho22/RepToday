## Per-pitch notes

### Pitch 1: Friction Is the Enemy - 8.85

The lowest-risk package on the table: it keeps the incumbent name and the planned listing name, so it introduces zero new naming decisions that would need defending, and it is the only pitch that explicitly flags the X/Instagram handle checks as inconclusive alongside the bolded UNVERIFIED trademark caveat.
Its collision reporting tracks `name-collisions.md` faithfully, including the "strong signal, not proof" framing on the NXDOMAIN result, and its performance claims ("under 100ms") are grounded in the brief rather than invented.
Specific weakness: keeping "Rep Today, Rest Tomorrow" preserves a pun that can be misread as never-rest intensity framing, and the pitch itself admits the perceived-speed wedge is partially imitable, so its 5-year positioning moat is the thinnest of the top three.
It also leans on named-competitor comparisons sourced to a competitors file outside the core evidence set, which is cited rather than invented but is the part of the pitch most exposed if a claim there is stale.

### Pitch 2: Identity and Discipline - 8.65

The strongest trademark posture of the four: the UNVERIFIED caveat is bolded, the pitch notes that App Store Connect reservation cannot even be checked pre-submission, and it uniquely tags the REP Fitness category-distance argument as an [ASSUMPTION] that only counsel can resolve.
The identity positioning is also the most durable five-year frame, since it is not tied to an imitable feature.
Specific weakness: the positioning statement asserts that Seven, Peloton, and Freeletics "punish a missed day" as streak-and-badge apps, a comparative disparagement the cited research does not substantiate (the streak-quit quotes are from Duolingo and habit apps), which is exactly the kind of unsupported competitor claim that draws FTC-style substantiation challenges.
It also lists Peloton as both a catalog app and a streak-and-badge app in the same sentence, and "Move Every Day" carries a permanent editorial burden the pitch itself concedes.

### Pitch 3: The Anti-Fitness-App - 7.10

The riskiest pitch despite carrying the required trademark caveat.
Its positioning statement names four competitors (Seven, Down Dog, Freeletics, 30 Day Fitness) and attributes streaks, badges, quizzes, and paywalls across them with only partial support in the provided research, and "Nothing pings her the next morning about what she owes" asserts a notification behavior the product brief never states, which is invented product evidence.
Calling the REP Fitness overlap "mindshare risks, not collision risks" pre-judges a trademark question the research explicitly leaves to counsel, and "squatted" is a loose legal characterization of an ordinary domain investor listing.
Enemy-of-the-category positioning also ages worst: the pitch itself admits "no streaks" is not ownable, and a brand defined by the category's pathologies loses its frame if the category cleans up.

### Pitch 4: Audience-First - 8.55

The best claims hygiene of the four: [ASSUMPTION] tags on every persona inference, per-pillar honesty notes, correct flagging that the vivid streak quotes are from Duolingo rather than fitness apps, and comparative claims aimed at generic categories rather than named competitors with unverified attributes.
The collision and trademark sections track the research accurately and carry the mandatory caveat plainly.
Specific weakness: the listing-name section contradicts itself ("Subtitle (30 chars)" followed by "(26 characters)" for the same string), and anchoring the store name to "5-Min" narrows the brand against a product that ships 5-60 minute sessions, a self-imposed constraint the brand must either honor or expensively shed within five years.
It is also slightly less careful than Pitches 1 and 2 about what the collision scan could not verify (social handles go unmentioned).

## Winner

**Pitch 1**, by a visible margin of **0.20** over Pitch 2 (8.85 vs 8.65), with Pitch 4 a further 0.10 back.

The margin is narrow and the reason is specific: on a pure risk rubric, the pitch that changes nothing it does not have to change, cites everything it claims, and flags every unverified channel wins, even though Pitch 2 has the more durable long-term frame and Pitch 4 the cleanest claims discipline.
No pitch violated the hard rules: all four kept Rep Today (respecting the completed migration), all four carried the UNVERIFIED trademark caveat, none made health claims, and none used streak or badge language positively.
