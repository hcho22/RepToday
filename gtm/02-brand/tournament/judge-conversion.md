# Judge: Conversion

Blind scoring of pitches 1-4 for Rep Today.
This judge scores one thing: will a cold stranger with zero social proof convert, and will the first run keep the promise that converted them.
All claims were checked against product-facts-brief.md, name-collisions.md, and review-mining.md.

## Published rubric (weights sum to 100)

| # | Criterion | Weight | Definition |
|---|-----------|--------|------------|
| 1 | Cold 5-second comprehension | 30 | From the hero (headline first, subhead as rescue), does a stranger who has never heard of the app know what it is and why they'd want it within 5 seconds. |
| 2 | Specificity without social proof | 25 | Claims are concrete, checkable, and believable on their own, since the product has zero users, zero reviews, and zero testimonials to lean on. |
| 3 | Promise-to-first-run proof | 25 | Does the exact hero promise get demonstrated within the first seconds of opening the app, so the first run instantly validates the ad that converted the user. |
| 4 | Listing scannability | 20 | App Store name + subtitle in one glance: category clarity, honest keywords, and a visible differentiator inside the 30-character limits. |

Penalty rule: any violation of product facts, invented evidence, health claims, positive streak/badge language, or an implied legal name clearance caps the offending criterion at 2 and is called out.
Result of the check: no pitch triggered a penalty.
All four stay inside product facts, cite only real research findings, avoid health claims, use streak language only negatively, and explicitly flag trademark clearance as UNVERIFIED.

## Per-pitch notes

### Pitch 1: Friction Is the Enemy - 8.65

"Open the app. Your workout is already there." is the most convertible hero in the field: a stranger understands the category and the differentiator in one read, and every subhead claim (under 100ms, no questions, no account, no internet) is a checkable product fact rather than a vibe.
It scores the only 10 on first-run proof because the product literally performs the headline on first open; the ad and the first session are the same event, which is the strongest possible substitute for social proof.
Specific weakness: it keeps "Rep Today, Rest Tomorrow" as the listing name, the weakest scannability choice of the four; the pun spends 13 characters carrying no category signal, and a cold browser gets the category only from the subtitle.
A second weakness the pitch itself admits: the emotional ceiling is low, and relief-over-aspiration is an unproven bet with zero users.

### Pitch 2: Identity and Discipline - 5.60

"Become someone who moves every day." fails the cold 5-second test: without the subhead it could be a habit tracker, a running app, or a journal, and identity promises are exactly the kind of claim a zero-user product cannot make believable.
It also fails first-run proof worst: becoming someone is unprovable at first open, and the forgiveness promise ("never zeroes you out") can only be verified after the user misses a day, weeks post-install.
The listing pair is genuinely good ("Rep Today: Move Every Day" + "Bodyweight workouts, no setup" carries clean keywords), which is why the score is not lower.
This is a strong retention story wearing an acquisition costume; it converts believers, not strangers.

### Pitch 3: The Anti-Fitness-App - 7.50

"The fitness app for people who hate fitness apps." is instantly parseable and names the category in the headline itself, but it is a well-worn formula that tells the stranger what the app is against before what it does, and it says little to a first-time downloader who has never been burned (a limit the pitch honestly concedes).
The subhead's negations (no quiz, no streaks, no locked workouts) are specific, but only the no-quiz half is provable at first run; no-streaks and no-locked-workouts take days to verify.
"Rep Today: Calm Home Workouts" + "Opens ready. No streaks, ever." scans well and carries real keywords, though "no streaks" is a meaningless phrase to the unburned segment of browsers.

### Pitch 4: Audience-First - 7.90

The listing pair is the best converter on the page: "Rep Today: 5-Min Home Workouts" + "No equipment. Opens ready." uses all 30 characters on the exact words a tired browser types and scans, and both subtitle claims are product facts.
The subhead is excellently specific (bodyweight only, 5 to 60 minutes, works offline) and maps well to first run.
Specific weakness: the headline "A floor, a wall, and five minutes." is evocative but ambiguous alone; a cold stranger needs the subhead to even identify the category, which costs it against Pitch 1's literal statement.
Secondary risk: anchoring the store name on "5-Min" invites misreading as a 7-minute-workout clone and undersells the 5-60 range, though 5 minutes counting as a full show-up keeps the claim honest.

## Winner and margin

**Winner: Pitch 1 (Friction Is the Enemy), 8.65.**
Margin: 0.75 points over Pitch 4 (7.90), with Pitch 3 at 7.50 and Pitch 2 at 5.60.
The verdict in one line: with zero social proof, the only ad this product can afford is one the first ten seconds of the app can prove, and Pitch 1 is the only pitch whose entire hero is provable on open.
Actionable note for the finalists: Pitch 1's hero grafted onto Pitch 4's listing name and subtitle would outscore every pitch as submitted.
