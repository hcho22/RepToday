# Judge: Conversion Lens

Tournament v2, blind independent judging.
Lens: conversion - what makes a cold, zero-context 9pm scroller stop, understand, believe, and install.
Reference reader throughout: a tired desk-bound adult at 9pm who has never heard of RepToday, has quit fitness apps before, and gives any ad 6 seconds.
Inputs read: the four pitches in this directory, `01-research/pain-point-frequency.md`, `01-research/meta-ad-library-sweep.md`, `01-research/name-collisions.md`, and `01-research/product-facts-brief.md` for claim verification.

## 1. Published rubric (fixed before scoring)

| # | Criterion | Weight | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | 6-second hook survival | 30 | Does the hook stop a cold scroller with zero brand context, without requiring a second sentence, a carried prior, or goodwill toward a contested word? Does the footage prove the line within the 6 seconds? |
| C2 | What-the-product-is clarity | 20 | After one screen (headline + subhead), does a stranger know this is a workout app, what it does, and why that is different? No decoding, no metaphor tax. |
| C3 | Immediately-verifiable promise | 20 | Does the lead stake itself on something the FIRST app open proves? A promise that pays off on open beats one that pays off in week two, because conversion happens before week two. |
| C4 | Match to top-ranked pains | 15 | Alignment with the frequency ranking: #1 paywall rage (7 mentions), #2 streak grief (6), #3 decision fatigue (4). Does the surface copy hit the pains people actually voiced, in something like their own words? |
| C5 | Freedom from faith-required claims | 15 | With zero users, zero ratings, zero testimonials, every claim a stranger must take on trust is a leak. Mechanics shown > mechanics asserted > concepts argued. Includes truth-policy and voice-rule exposure. |

Weights sum to 100.

## 3. Per-pitch judgments

### Pitch 1: The App Is the Disciplined One (64.0, rank 4)

Strongest thing: the proof line and demo dependency are exactly right - "Open to Start: zero questions, zero setup, a full session on screen in under 100ms" is the best single always-in-viewport line in the tournament, and every mechanic cited checks against product-facts-brief.md.
Weakest thing: the hook asks a cold scroller to accept an inversion of a contested word in one sentence, and the pitch's own risk section concedes that in any truncated surface the word alone miscodes the brand toward the Jillian Michaels drill-sergeant pole, which is the only place a 9pm scroller has ever seen "discipline" in a fitness ad (meta-ad-library-sweep.md).
The headline "The app is the disciplined one" tells a stranger nothing about what the product is until the subhead rescues it, which spends the one screen C2 measures.
Flag (soft, truth policy): the positioning line "carries the discipline for you" edges toward promising a behavioral outcome no app can deliver; the pitch names this risk itself, but a lead whose natural compressions keep landing on its own banned reading is paying a permanent editing tax.
This is the best BRAND idea in the tournament and the worst cold-conversion instrument, which is the exact trade my rubric punishes.

### Pitch 2: Anti-Discipline / Already Ready (88.5, rank 1)

Strongest thing: the hook makes no claim the footage does not prove within two seconds - "Every workout app asks how long you have. This one doesn't ask anything," cut to a session simply existing - and the headline "Open the app. The workout is already there." makes the product legible to a stranger in eight words with zero decoding.
It is also the only pitch whose top three pillars map one-to-one onto the top three ranked pains (decision fatigue, paywall rage, streak grief) in the users' own vocabulary, which pain-point-frequency.md notes is "decision, clicks, scrolling," not "discipline."
Weakest thing: by its own honest admission it has no ownable identity sentence and sells the start rather than the staying, so it buys installs and must rebuild the emotional arc downstream; a competitor can utter "no setup" tomorrow, and the only moat is the live demo.
Flag (soft, truth policy): "Every workout app asks how long you have" is an absolute a stranger can falsify with one counterexample (Seven, NTC); the tested variants ("This workout app has no setup. Watch.") avoid the absolute and should be preferred.
Everything else survives verification: no-questions open, under-100ms regeneration, offline, free unlimited core, and the in-session swap it uses to answer the control-seeker objection are all confirmed product facts.

### Pitch 3: Showing Up Is the Whole Game (69.0, rank 3)

Strongest thing: "I skipped four days and my workout app congratulated me for coming back" is the single best story-hook sentence in the tournament - a complete violated expectation in one line, needing no brand trust.
Weakest thing: the whole lead pays off at the viewer's first missed day, i.e. week two, while conversion happens at second six; the first app open proves the ready session, but this pitch deliberately demotes that to pillar 3, so its C3 promise ("nothing can ever zero it") is checkable only after a week of use.
The headline "Showing up is the whole game" could sit on a therapy app, a sales course, or a poker newsletter; category clarity arrives only in the subhead.
The pitch also concedes the contract "has nothing to push against" for viewers without streak-grief history, which shrinks the cold audience my lens is scored on, and its central evidence transfer (Duolingo grief to fitness) is flagged by the research itself as a bet.
Flag (hard, truth policy): the hook sentence is first-person past-tense experience from a product with zero users; voiced by anyone but the founder-on-camera-with-the-screen-record it becomes a fabricated testimonial, so the founder framing is load-bearing and must be non-negotiable in every cut; relatedly, "this app never makes you feel behind" is an experience guarantee no mechanic can enforce and should never ship as copy.

### Pitch 4: Contrarian / Nothing to Lose (75.0, rank 2)

Strongest thing: "A workout app with nothing to lose" is the best headline in the tournament for a cold reader - it names the category, carries a true double meaning, and is literally verifiable inside the free app in thirty seconds; the pillar set covers all three top pains with cited receipts.
Weakest thing: the hook is slow for the format - a 25-word two-sentence opener whose promised payoff ("watch what happens when I miss a day") cannot actually be demonstrated live in six seconds, only simulated with a score-bending screen; the friction backup hook is stronger and closer to Pitch 2's.
Negative-space positioning also needs the villain pre-recognized, which narrows the cold audience the same way Pitch 3 does, and the pitch's own risk list admits the refusal is copyable as copy.
Flag (soft, truth policy): the framing claim that competitors' machinery "serves the app's retention curve, not the user's consistency" is an unfalsifiable motive attribution a stranger must take on faith; it is fine as internal strategy but must stay out of shipped copy, and the pitch's guardrail on the out-of-category Duolingo quotes is correctly load-bearing.
Flag (voice, watch closely): a refusal register sits one sloppy edit from the oppositional "anti-fitness-app" gag the voice rules' calm-plain-declarative standard cannot survive; the pitch names this gag-collapse risk itself.

## 4. The discipline question

Drop "discipline" from the surface; keep it as the internal spine only.
The conversion case is one-sided once you hold the 9pm scroller constant.
First, the prior: the only discipline-register creative observed in the entire ad sweep is drill-sergeant ("your no-excuses solution"), so a zero-equity brand leading with the word inherits that reading and must spend its only 6 seconds arguing out of it.
Second, the vocabulary: nobody in the pain corpus asks for discipline; they ask for "tell me exactly what to do," rage at paywalls, and grieve streaks, and matching customer vocabulary is the cheapest credibility a zero-proof brand can buy.
Third, the format: Pitch 1's inversion genuinely works at paragraph length, but every 6-second surface truncates it back to the bare word, and a lead word that is only safe with a chaperone sentence is not a lead word.
Fourth, targeting: on TikTok and Shorts the creative is the targeting, and "discipline" selects the intensity audience this product deliberately does not serve.
The spine should remain fully intact internally - in the product story, retention copy, manifesto, and founder interviews, where the inversion has room to land - and Pitch 1's hook is worth one cheap creative test later, because if the flat-register attribution-to-the-object reading does land on strangers, it is the most ownable sentence anyone wrote; but the launch surface should lead with the demonstration, not the word.

## 5. Name verdict

RepToday stands, and under a conversion rubric it stands more firmly than under any other lens.
The name adds zero explanation cost to the six seconds: two concrete syllable-cheap nouns that name the smallest unit of doing and the only day the app cares about, requiring no metaphor decode from a stranger - which is exactly the property the winning demonstration-led pitch needs, since every second spent decoding the name is a second stolen from the footage that is the only proof this pre-launch brand owns.
The collision scan supports it as the cleanest candidate: no "Rep Today" variant in US App Store search, reptoday.app NXDOMAIN, github handle open, and the main web collision (REP Fitness) is an equipment retailer, not an app; both challengers fail my rubric outright - Cairn requires a metaphor explanation a 6-second hook cannot afford and has a live same-category Health & Fitness collision, and Stack is saturated past findability (exact-name game, 56K+ ratings) while its accumulation semantics are streak-shaped, the very mental model the product refutes.
The one real conversion liability is the strength-coded "Rep" prefix crowd (RepCount and kin), which can misread as a rep counter until the subtitle corrects it - and that makes the unanimous four-pitch flag correct and urgent: kill the draft listing suffix "Rest Tomorrow," which is grind-coded, streak-adjacent, and wastes the subtitle slot, and spend that slot on the verifiable mechanic instead (something in the register of "Rep Today: Workouts, Ready"), pending ASO character-limit and keyword checks.
Trademark and App Store clearance remain unverified for every candidate and stay the founder's next action.

## 6. Bottom line

Winner under the conversion lens: Pitch 2 (Anti-Discipline / Already Ready), 88.5, by a 13.5-point margin.
Runner-up: Pitch 4, whose "nothing to lose" headline and free-app verifiability are strong enough to borrow as a supporting layer.
Best single hook sentence: Pitch 3's, usable later with mandatory founder framing.
Best brand idea, wrong launch surface: Pitch 1.
Discipline: internal spine only.
Name: RepToday confirmed; replace the "Rest Tomorrow" listing suffix with the ready-on-open mechanic.
