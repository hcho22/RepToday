# Judge: Differentiation Lens

Tournament v2, blind positioning + name tournament.
Judge lens: differentiation.
Reference reader: a 9pm scroller with zero context, zero brand trust, and a decade of fitness-app priors.
All scores were assigned after the rubric was fixed and before any pitch was re-read for advocacy.
Research checked directly: `01-research/meta-ad-library-sweep.md`, `01-research/pain-point-frequency.md`, `01-research/name-collisions.md`, `01-research/product-facts-brief.md`, `01-research/competitors-additional.md`, `01-research/competitor-down-dog.md`.

## 1. Published rubric (fixed before scoring)

| # | Criterion | Weight | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Territory ownability | 30 | Is the claimed lead territory actually unoccupied per the ad-library sweep's crowded/unoccupied map, and can a zero-equity brand plausibly plant a durable flag there, in language and in mechanics? |
| 2 | Distance from nearest live message | 20 | Word-level and register-level distance from the closest observed live competitor creative. Word-level proximity is penalized even when the meaning is opposite, because the 9pm scroller reads words, not intentions. |
| 3 | Imitation cost | 20 | How cheaply can an incumbent copy the words versus the mechanics? A lead that forces the mechanics into the frame scores high; a lead any competitor can utter in tomorrow's ad scores low. |
| 4 | Category legibility | 20 | Can the cold scroller place the product as a workout app and grasp the claim inside 6 seconds? A lead so novel the product cannot be placed is a differentiation FAILURE, not a win. |
| 5 | Truth and voice under compression | 10 | Does the differentiated claim survive truncation to its shortest form without violating truth policy, voice rules, or product facts, and without miscoding into an adjacent competitor's register? |

Weights sum to 100.

## 3. Per-pitch judgments

### Pitch 1: The app is the disciplined one (rank 4, 6.50)

Strongest: the imitation cost of its central sentence is the highest in the tournament, because "the app is the disciplined one" is only speakable by an app that opens ready, has no streaks, and gates nothing, so Freeletics cannot copy it while running badge ads.
Weakest: it fails my lens's word-distance test, because the only live discipline creative in the sweep is Jillian Michaels' drill-sergeant "no-excuses" ad, and the pitch's own risk section concedes that any surface that truncates the inversion to the single word relocates the brand into that lot.
A lead word that the pitch itself says "does real work only when the inversion lands" and needs the chaperone sentence in the same breath is, per criterion 5, not a lead word for a zero-equity brand.
Truth flags: none hard; the pitch is honest about small-N decision-fatigue evidence and correctly attributes every product claim to the facts brief.
Soft voice flag: "the app supplies the discipline" sits one careless paraphrase from a behavioral promise the app cannot keep, which the pitch itself names as overpromise risk.

### Pitch 2: Anti-discipline, already-ready (rank 1, 7.30)

Strongest: it plants its flag exactly on the ground the sweep maps as unoccupied (ready-on-open, friction-deletion, the screen-record demo with "no observed competition"), and it is the only pitch whose 6-second hook makes zero claims the footage does not immediately prove, which is maximum legibility for a scroller with no context.
Weakest: imitation cost, and the pitch admits it in its own words ("the claim is cheap to say and only expensive to build" and "the lead has no moat sentence"); Peloton's live "Less time choosing classes, more time moving" ad proves a giant is already standing one lane over, and any configure-first app can utter "no setup" falsely tomorrow.
Its adjacency to the single most crowded observed lane (time compression) is a permanent editorial tax the pitch names honestly.
Truth flags: none; every cited competitor characterization checks out against the teardowns (Down Dog's type/length/level/music/voice configuration gauntlet, Seven and Bend's streaks and gated free tiers, Peloton's hardware attachment).
This pitch wins my lens not because its territory is the most defensible but because it is the only one that occupies mapped-empty ground while staying instantly placeable, and differentiation that the scroller cannot decode is not differentiation.

### Pitch 3: Showing up is the whole game (rank 2, 7.20)

Strongest: its hook ("I skipped four days and my workout app congratulated me for coming back") is the single most differentiated line in the tournament, a one-sentence expectation violation on ground the sweep confirms is empty ("zero observed ads mention missed days kindly"), and its insistence on affirmative mechanics over absence claims ("congratulated", not "didn't punish") is the correct answer to the copyability problem.
Weakest: the headline collides at word level with a live competitor ad, because Jillian Michaels is running "All you have to do is show up" right now, and the pitch's "reclamation" framing requires exactly the context a 6-second cut deletes; the hook is far from everything observed, but the headline is near the enemy.
Second weakness under my lens: negative-space forgiveness only lands on viewers who have felt streak grief, which the pitch concedes narrows the wedge to app-quitters, and the anti-streak language is already being claimed by indie builders in organic channels.
Truth flags: none hard; the Duolingo-quote guardrail is handled correctly and the evidence-transfer bet is flagged as an assumption, which is the honest treatment.
If the headline were rebuilt to keep the hook's distance, this pitch would likely take rank 1 on my rubric.

### Pitch 4: Contrarian, the refusal productized (rank 3, 6.80)

Strongest: it has the greatest register distance from the observed field of any pitch, because the auction literally advertises badges and challenges (Freeletics' badge-prize ad) while this lead advertises their absence, and its missed-day demo is footage no observed competitor can shoot.
Weakest: its sharpest words are the cheapest to copy, since "no streaks" is an afternoon's work for any competitor's copywriter, and the pitch's own risk list concedes the moat is "a head start, not a wall"; meanwhile "a workout app with nothing to lose" risks parsing as generic risk-free-trial talk to a cold scroller, and the refusal needs the villain recognized to land at all.
The oppositional register also spends calm-brand equity the voice rules exist to protect, which the pitch admits, and one sloppy execution collapses it into an anti-fitness-app gag.
Truth flags: none hard; the 57-movement library, Asymmetric Ramp, and pricing claims all match the facts brief, competitor attacks are cited to teardowns, and the pitch honestly concedes the attacked incumbents' genuine strengths.
Soft flag: the hero subhead's "No paywall in front of the workout" must never run near Nike Training Club targeting, per the research guardrail all four pitches acknowledge.

## 4. The discipline question

"Discipline" should be the internal spine only, not the surface lead word.
The differentiation evidence is one-directional.
First, the only live creative occupying discipline vocabulary is the drill-sergeant pole ("no-excuses", per the sweep), so leading with the word puts a zero-equity brand at near-zero word-distance from its own anti-thesis, which is the worst possible score on my criterion 2.
Second, the pain corpus shows nobody asks for discipline in their own words; the complaint vocabulary is decisions, resets, and paywalls, so the word buys no recognition from the reference reader.
Third, the inversion that makes the word safe requires a second sentence, and every 6-second surface deletes second sentences, so the word fails truth-and-voice-under-compression structurally, not accidentally.
Dropping it entirely would be an overcorrection: the product's two-phase journey literally names a Discipline Phase, the strategy documents are organized around structural discipline, and pitch 1's inversion is genuinely ownable at essay length, so the word earns its keep in the manifesto, retention copy, founder interviews, and product architecture.
Spine, not surface.

## 5. Name verdict

RepToday stands, and no challenger comes close under a differentiation rubric.
On ownability, the collision scan shows no "Rep Today" or close variant in US App Store search, an NXDOMAIN on reptoday.app, and a main web collision (REP Fitness) that is an equipment retailer rather than an app, which is the cleanest flag-planting position of any candidate.
On distance, the crowded "Rep"-prefix neighborhood is gym-logging trackers (RepCount, RepCounter Pro), and "Today" is precisely the token that separates the name from every one of them, so the collision risk is a subtitle-fixable misread rather than a territorial conflict.
On legibility, my lens penalizes names so novel the scroller cannot place the product, and both alternates fail exactly there or worse: Cairn needs its metaphor explained and carries a live same-category App Store collision (a differentiation killer, since a shared name in the same store category is zero distance), and Stack is saturated beyond findability (exact-name game with 56K+ ratings) while its accumulation semantics are streak-shaped, actively counter-positioned against the forgiveness mechanics every pitch leads with.
RepToday is plain, two concrete syllables, names the category's smallest unit plus the only day the app scores, and all four blind pitches independently confirmed it, which is the strongest convergence signal in the tournament.
One unanimous and correct flag I endorse: the draft listing suffix "Rest Tomorrow" drifts toward the crowded grind register the brand differentiates against and should be replaced with a mechanic-bearing subtitle; and formal trademark plus App Store Connect clearance remain UNVERIFIED for every candidate and are the founder's next action.
