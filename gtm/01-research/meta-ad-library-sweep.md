# Meta Ad Library sweep: fitness-app creative (US, active ads)

Task R2 for the RepToday go-to-market v2 update.
Fetch date: 2026-08-01.
Method: the public Meta Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library) returned HTTP 403 to plain HTTP fetching, so every search below was loaded in a real rendered browser session with no login, and the visible ad cards were read directly from the page.
All searches used country=US, active ads only, keyword search, sorted by total impressions.
A keyword search matches ad text and advertiser names, not a page's full inventory, so per-advertiser coverage is partial by construction; this whole document is bounded by what was observable that way.
Every ad text quoted below was read verbatim from a loaded Ad Library results page listed in "Sources fetched".

## Per-advertiser findings (verified by direct observation)

### Freeletics - VERIFIED (~37 active US-visible results)
Nearly the entire visible active set is one concept farmed into many versions: "Get your personalized Training Coach and get in the best shape ever!" with the bullet list "Daily instructions / Effective workouts / Easy meal plans," in English and German, closing on "Freeletics: Europe's #1 Fitness App."
Formats are short videos (9-28 seconds) plus statics, all driving to freeletics.com with a Learn More CTA.
Two branded-content influencer ads (page "stell_estela with Freeletics") run a softer angle: a 2-week "#FindYourFree Challenge" community push with an in-app badge and prize partners, and a "Strong enough to train. Free enough to enjoy" lifestyle piece tied to Tomorrowland.
Angles observed: personalization/AI coach, geographic authority claim, community challenge, lifestyle-balance.
Notably, the challenge ad dangles an in-app badge as the reward, which is exactly the gamification RepToday refuses.

### Peloton - VERIFIED (searched "onepeloton"; ~59 results, most from Peloton itself)
Peloton is running a wide, fresh flight (most ads started Jul 28-31, 2026).
Angles observed: price reframing ("Skip the per-class fees - unlock daily wins with one monthly Peloton All-Access Membership"), instructor charisma ("Flow with instructors who breathe life into every rep"), financing ("$275/mo for 12 months"), 30-day home trial, new retail stores, motivation/identity ("Your motivation, our momentum... find your why"), and community discounts ("Up to $600 off... for our everyday icons").
One ad is directly adjacent to RepToday's wedge: "Less time choosing classes, more time moving. Peloton IQ guides your routine and a 30-day home trial makes it easy to start."
That is a choice-reduction claim, but it is attached to a $1,000+ hardware purchase and an AI-recommendation frame, not to an instant ready-on-open session.
Formats: 10-47 second product videos, statics, one influencer branded-content ad ("@onepeloton helps you rediscover the joy in movement," with a creator page).

### Sweat / Kayla Itsines - VERIFIED (searched "Kayla Itsines"; ~73 results, overwhelmingly her own ads)
The most template-driven advertiser observed: short 12-16 second videos with a bullet-list body and a "Start your 7-day FREE trial today!" close on nearly every ad.
Angles observed: 30-minute cap as headline value ("Strength in 30," "Every workout is capped at 30 minutes"), explicit anti-intensity positioning ("No HIIT. No jumping. Just pure strength training," "Contains ZERO burpees"), zero equipment ("Try my Zero Equipment program... No equipment necessary!"), small-space training ("just the size of a yoga mat"), busy-person framing ("Your busy schedule 🤝 Strength in 30"), pregnancy/postpartum programs, annual-plan price anchoring ("from $11.25/month"), and an exercise-swap feature ("Auto-swaps for any exercises you don't like").
A separate Sweat brand-page ad (caught in the "7 minute workout" search) pushes a "Precision Pilates" program: "No jumping. No rushing. Just precision."
This is the crowd RepToday's zero-equipment and time-respect claims will stand next to; Sweat already owns "zero equipment + short + no punishment" language loudly.

### Ladder - VERIFIED (searched "joinladder"; ~520 results)
The heaviest observed spender of the set, running a whitelisted-creator UGC machine: many ads come from creator handle pages like "melissafromladder," "kendallfromladder," "zoefromladder," "Juliannafromladder" paired "with Ladder."
Dominant angle is physique outcome: "Strength training engineered to sculpt your curves into an hourglass physique with barbells, dumbbells and machines."
Second angle is celebrity social proof: multiple Hilary Duff ads ("I shifted to strength training, and everything changed for me," "join Hilary Duff and 400,000 strong women on Ladder"), including a 2-minute coach-interviews-celebrity video.
Consistency appears only as a supporting claim inside the Hilary Duff copy ("Having a plan on Ladder keeps Hilary Duff consistent in the gym"), never as the headline promise.
Proof stack on almost every ad: "4.9 stars +100K reviews," "2025 Apple App of the Year Finalist," "7-day free trial, no credit card required."
Formats: 6-40 second UGC talking-head and gym b-roll videos.

### Caliber - VERIFIED (searched "caliberstrong"; ~20 results, all Caliber)
Two concepts only.
One: third-party endorsement as the entire hook ("'Best Free Workout App of 2026' - Garage Gym Reviews" and "'2026 Best Free Workout App' - Men's Journal") on 9-25 second videos with a Download CTA and "Unlimited Free Workout Tracking."
Two: paid coaching ("Look and feel your best with online personal training / Personal training built around you").
Free-forever as an acquisition hook is therefore already in-market, but framed as a tracking tool award, not as a discipline system.

### Centr - VERIFIED with a caveat (searched "Centr"; ~570 results, mostly unrelated "central/centre" businesses)
Every Centr-page ad visible in the top results sells HYROX equipment, not the app: "Equip it with the only complete HYROX training solution," "Centr x HYROX Sandbag... Train now. Pay later with Shop Pay," aimed partly at gym facilities.
No app-subscription creative from Centr surfaced in the observable slice; that absence is a property of this keyword slice, not proof Centr runs no app ads.

### pliability - VERIFIED (searched "pliability"; ~310 results, most from pliability itself)
The closest in-market neighbor to RepToday's mobility-as-relief pillar, and it is well covered.
Angles observed: normal-person framing ("Simple mobility training designed for real people, not just athletes"), loss-aversion urgency ("Your body is losing range of motion right now"), desk-worker resets ("Your workflow is missing one thing. Short mobility resets built into your work-from-home routine"), recovery ROI ("The Best ROI in Fitness," "Stretch Smarter, Not Longer"), runner injury-causality education ("Most running injuries don't start where they hurt"), pregnancy mobility, a 3-minute mobility test funnel, volume proof ("1,700+ routines. New videos daily"), athlete authority (Tia-Clair Toomey branded-content ad), and "Free 7-Day Trial. Cancel anytime. No card."
Formats: 13-44 second videos plus multi-card carousels of site links.
Note: pliability sells mobility as performance/recovery infrastructure for people who already train; nobody in its observed set sells mobility as the workout itself for a discipline-first general user.

### GOWOD - VERIFIED (searched "GOWOD"; ~8 results, all GOWOD)
Pure elite-athlete authority positioning: "Aimee Cringle is the 2026 Fittest Woman on Earth. She trains her mobility with GOWOD," "Recover Like Tom Evans," "The System Used By Champions."
Assessment-led funnel: "One test = One custom program," "Gain 18% in mobility in just 60 days with GOWOD Premium," and a challenge hook ("Can you beat the average runner? We tested 33,000+ runners who scored an average of 62%...").
Proof: "Rated 4.9 from 1.6M+ users."
Time claim: mobility "in less than 10 minutes" built into a runner's day.
Formats: 16-56 second videos, athlete branded content, Download CTA.

## Synthesis: crowded vs. unoccupied territory

Bounded honestly: this is what was observable in one logged-out US keyword sweep on 2026-08-01, not a census.

Crowded creative territory (do not fight here head-on):
Time compression as the headline: 7 minutes, 15 minutes, 30-minute caps, "more in less time" (Sweat, HOTWORX, Jillian Michaels, One&Done, GOWOD).
Personalization/AI-coach claims: Freeletics, Built With Science, Peloton IQ, pliability, GOWOD, Yoga-Go.
7-day free trial with no credit card as the offer: Sweat, Ladder, pliability, GOWOD; 14-30 day trials from Built With Science and Peloton.
Ratings-and-awards proof stacks: 4.9 stars, 100K reviews, App of the Year, "Best Free Workout App" awards (Ladder, Caliber, GOWOD).
Celebrity and elite-athlete authority: Hilary Duff, Tia-Clair Toomey, Aimee Cringle, Tom Evans.
Whitelisted-creator UGC talking-heads at volume: Ladder above all.
Physique-outcome promises: hourglass, sculpt, visible transformation in 28 days (Ladder, Yoga-Go, BetterMe).
Challenge mechanics with badges and prizes: Freeletics, BetterMe, Nandy Yoga.
Quiz and assessment funnels: Built With Science, One&Done, GOWOD, pliability's mobility test.

Territory relevant to RepToday's wedge that appeared UNOCCUPIED in what was observed:
Ready-on-open / zero-decision start: nobody demonstrates "open the app, a session is already there."
Peloton's "less time choosing classes" is the nearest neighbor, and it resolves the problem with an AI recommender on hardware, not instant deterministic readiness; a screen-record ad of a session existing in under a second has no observed competition.
Friction-deletion as the story: every observed ad sells workout content or coaching; none sells the deletion of the setup ritual (no "how long do you have?", no plan-picking, no download-wait, offline).
Forgiveness and anti-streak: zero observed ads mention missed days kindly.
The observed motivational register is either drill-sergeant ("no-excuses," "All you have to do is show up") or challenge-streak mechanics; "a consistency score that forgives you" is unclaimed language in this slice.
Anti-gamification explicitly: Freeletics literally advertises badges; no one advertises their absence.
Mobility as relief for regular people who do NOT already train: pliability's "real people, not just athletes" line gestures at it but its observed set is dominated by recovery-for-athletes and runner-performance angles, and GOWOD is entirely athlete-first; "mobility so your back stops hurting, as a co-equal pillar of the workout itself" is open.
Zero-equipment as identity rather than feature bullet: Sweat uses it as one bullet among many; no observed advertiser builds the whole creative on "your body is the gym, forever free."
Honest caveat: unoccupied-in-observation is weaker evidence than crowded-in-observation; a logged-out keyword sweep can miss an advertiser's whole inventory, dark posts, and TikTok/YouTube creative entirely.

## Implications for RepToday creative testing (opinion, derived from the above)

The observed auction rewards a proof element (rating, award, athlete) and a zero-risk offer line; RepToday launches with neither, so its substitute must be the demo itself - screen-record of app-open to ready-session is a format nobody observed is using and is intrinsically believable.
[ASSUMPTION] A "we deleted the part you hate" negative-space ad (no streaks, no badges, no setup quiz) would read as contrarian against the observed field, because every neighboring advertiser was seen selling exactly those mechanics; this is an inference from the creative gap, not a tested result.
Avoid leading with "7 minutes" or "30 minutes" alone; the time-compression lane is the single most crowded observed message.
