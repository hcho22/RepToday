# Additional Direct Competitors: Teardown

Research date: 2026-07-15 (all fetches 2026-07-15 UTC; prices and features are as shown on the fetched pages that day).
Scope: competitors closest to Rep Today's actual shape (zero-equipment bodyweight, micro sessions, mobility, habit-first), excluding the already-covered list (Down Dog, Freeletics, NTC, Ladder, Sweat, Caliber, Peloton, Apple Fitness+).

## TL;DR

Four apps sit closest to Rep Today's wedge: Seven (7-minute bodyweight, mass market, heavily gamified), Bend (daily stretching habit, streaks, paywalled free tier), Wakeout (desk micro-movement, lowest session-start friction found anywhere), and pliability (mobility for athletes, notably gamification-free).
No competitor combines all three of Rep Today's differentiators: an already-generated session on open, a forgiving non-streak consistency metric, and an unlimited free core loop.
Seven is closest in workout shape but is built on exactly the streak-and-achievement mechanics Rep Today rejects.
Onyx: Home Workout, a former AI bodyweight competitor, appears delisted (its App Store URL returned 404 on 2026-07-15), so it was dropped from the teardown.

## 1. Seven: 7 Minute Workout (Perigee)

Source: [App Store page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/seven-7-minute-workout/id650276551), [seven.app](https://seven.app/), both fetched 2026-07-15.

**Positioning.** "Daily HIIT Bodyweight Exercise" with science-based 7-minute workouts, no equipment, personalized plans for goals (Get Fit, Lose Weight, Get Strong).
The site tagline is "Small habits lead to big changes" and claims over 30 million users and a 4.7-star rating from 600,000+ reviews.
The US App Store page showed 4.8 stars from 137K ratings and an Editors' Choice award.

**Pricing / free tier.** The App Store in-app purchase list showed 7 Club Membership Monthly at $9.99 and Annual at $79.99, plus legacy one-time purchases ($39.99-$59.99 lifetime tier, $9.99 packs).
Free download gives basic access; premium unlocks the 200+ exercise library and personalized coaching.

**Session-start friction.** Fixed 7-minute format with goal-based plan selection; the app is organized around a daily workout rather than an open browse library.

**Gamification.** Heavy: streaks with daily challenge tracking, an achievement ladder ("novice to athlete"), a 7-month challenge with milestone rewards, leaderboard-style friend competition, and a rule allowing up to 3 skip days per month without breaking the streak.
That skip-day mechanic is Seven's partial concession to the same brittleness Rep Today's non-streak Consistency Score is designed around.

**Review themes.** Streaks as motivation ("I just reached 105 days, it helps me stay motivated") and injury modifications ("the only app I have found that checked all the boxes"), per review excerpts on the fetched App Store page.

## 2. Bend: Stretching & Flexibility (Bowery Digital)

Source: [App Store page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bend-stretching-flexibility/id1513988468), [bend.com](https://bend.com/), [Bustle review](https://www.bustle.com/wellness/bend-stretching-app-review-price-features-subscriptions), all fetched 2026-07-15.

**Positioning.** "Where movement begins" - "the simple, affordable way to make stretching & movement a part of your daily routine," for all ages, explicitly including over-40 users, post-injury users, and office workers.
The site claims over 15 million users and 450,000+ reviews; the US App Store page showed 4.8 stars from 171K ratings.

**Pricing / free tier.** App Store IAP list showed monthly membership at $4.99-$13.99 and annual at $19.99-$39.99 across tiers; the Bustle review priced full access at $13.99/mo or $39.99/yr.
Per Bustle, the free tier after trial is a single 5-minute "Wake Up" flow of 8 static stretches - "Most of the app is behind a paywall."

**Session-start friction.** Onboarding questionnaire on first launch (goals, experience, limitations); after that, short daily routines with reminders.

**Gamification.** Streaks tracking consecutive days plus a "Bendometer" consistency dashboard the app suggests keeping above 90%.
It is a habit app, but a streak-based one: exactly the loss-framed mechanic Rep Today avoids.

**Review themes.** Motivation and consistency plus sleek simplicity ("as sleek and user-friendly as they come" per Bustle; "It's awesome because it really motivates you to stretch" per an App Store review excerpt), against repetitiveness and the paywalled free tier as the main complaints.

## 3. Wakeout! Break the Sit Habit (Wakeout, LLC)

Source: [App Store page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wakeout-exercise-breaks/id1242116567), [wakeout.app](https://wakeout.app/), both fetched 2026-07-15.

**Positioning.** "Tiny, goofy, uncoordinated exercises you do right at your desk," with the anti-positioning tagline "Not a fitness app. Please."
Targets desk-bound remote workers, engineers, teachers, and parents; 2,000+ no-equipment movements; former Apple App of the Year and Editors' Choice.
US App Store page showed 4.5 stars from 8.4K ratings.

**Pricing / free tier.** App Store showed IAP from $4.99 to $69.99 with a $59.99 annual subscription and a 7-day free trial; limited access without subscribing.
The Chrome extension is free.

**Session-start friction.** The lowest measured in this research: the site claims "Four taps. Under 30 seconds" to start a movement, with a 3-minute average session, and it reads sedentary periods from Apple Health instead of arbitrary timers.
This is the closest existing benchmark to Rep Today's open-to-ready-session promise, though Wakeout still asks outcome, location, and position before starting.

**Gamification.** Playful rather than competitive: "Wakeout Watts" points, an Active Pass that locks favorite apps until a 1-minute movement is done, daily goals, and companion characters.
One fetched review: "Earning Wakeout watts which feels like imaginary points in a game is oddly so rewarding."

**Review themes.** Barrier-lowering for ADHD and chronic-pain users ("This app lowers the barrier of entry to move more often"; "keeps me from getting as stiff, and lowers my pain"), with some UI confusion among older users.

## 4. pliability: Stretch & Mobility (pliability, LLC)

Source: [App Store page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pliability-stretch-mobility/id1175346453), fetched 2026-07-15.

**Positioning.** Formerly ROMWOD; "Official stretching partner of HYROX"; guided mobility and recovery for athletes, fusing mobility, yoga, prehab, and recovery.
US App Store page showed 4.8 stars from 9,700+ ratings.

**Pricing / free tier.** Athlete Monthly $19.99, Athlete Annual $179.99, with a 7-day trial and no meaningful free tier - the premium-priced end of this set.

**Session-start friction.** Daily 12-20 minute routines, no equipment; a Mobility Test drives personalized recommendations.

**Gamification.** None visible on the fetched page - no streaks, no badges - making it the only competitor here that shares Rep Today's anti-gamification stance, though by omission rather than as a stated philosophy.

**Review themes.** Real physical results ("deep seated kinks in my neck were released") versus price-sensitivity and quality complaints ("This is expensive enough of an app that I shouldn't be having inconsistent sessions") and refund friction ("The company does not offer refunds").

## Who is actually closest to Rep Today's wedge, ranked

1. **Seven.** Same workout shape (zero-equipment bodyweight micro sessions), same busy mass-market audience, same daily-habit frame, and enormous scale (30M+ claimed users) proving the demand.
It is the app Rep Today must be differentiated against, and the differentiation is clean: Seven's engine is streaks, achievements, and friend leaderboards, while Rep Today bans all of it and replaces the streak with a forgiving Consistency Score.
Seven's 3-skip-days-per-month rule is evidence the market already feels streak brittleness.
2. **Bend.** Closest on the habit-first philosophy and the desk-bound over-30 audience, and its 15M+ claimed users validate "small daily movement" as a category.
But it covers only Rep Today's mobility pillar, uses streaks plus a Bendometer, and its free tier is one 5-minute routine versus Rep Today's unlimited free workouts.
3. **Wakeout.** Closest on session-start friction ("Four taps. Under 30 seconds") and on the tired-desk-worker persona, but it deliberately opts out of being a fitness app: no strength progression, no adaptive difficulty, sessions average 3 minutes.
Rep Today occupies the space between Wakeout's frictionlessness and Seven's actual training.
4. **pliability.** Overlaps Rep Today's co-primary mobility pillar and shares the no-gamification trait, but its athlete/recovery positioning, 12-20 minute format, and $179.99/yr price aim at a different buyer than a tired parent at 9pm.

**The open gap all four confirm:** nobody pairs (a) a session already on screen at open, (b) a miss-forgiving non-streak consistency metric, and (c) an unlimited free core loop.
Seven and Bend monetize by gating the library; Rep Today's never-gate-the-core-loop free tier is a direct attack on Bend's most-complained-about weakness.
